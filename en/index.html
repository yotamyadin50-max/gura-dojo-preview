What happens in a class?

The class is built as a gradual process: from listening inward, through movement and physical work, to meeting another person and to the struggle. At the end we return to observation and let the experience become learning.

  1. 01

    Gathering and listening

    We begin by stopping and looking inward. We turn our attention to the breath, to the sensations of the body, and to the state in which we arrived: physically and mentally. From that listening we begin to build a relationship with the body and to be present to what is happening in us and around us.

  2. 02

    Warm-up and free movement

    Through gradual physical practice we wake the body and prepare it for the work. We move on the mat at different levels, learn to roll, change direction, and move freely in the space. The warm-up is also a chance to develop creativity, play, and the ability to respond spontaneously and intuitively.

  3. 03

    Learning technique and principles

    We learn techniques of safe falling, orientation in space, and principles from mixed martial arts. Through the practice we develop coordination, stability, timing, and the ability to use the body efficiently. The work is adapted to each person’s abilities, experience, and physical condition.

  4. 04

    Mixed martial arts

    Here we enter the martial art through practical practice. We practice throws, releases from grips, control of space, and keeping balance.

    Through wrestling and sumo games, in which the aim is to take a partner off balance, to submit them, or to push them beyond the edge of the mat, we learn to apply force, to see openings, to time the action, and to respond to the opponent’s movement.

  5. 05

    Return, processing, and integration

    After the physical work we return to the breath, to the sensations of the body, and to listening inward, as at the start of class. We make room for the experience we have been through, notice what arose in us, and look at what we learned about ourselves through meeting the other.

    We leave the mat with a real bodily experience of meeting a challenge, and with a chance to take something from it into the rest of the day: more awareness, confidence in the body, a sense of capability, and the ability to meet ourselves and our surroundings in a more flexible and attentive way.

What do we mean when we talk about martial arts?

Martial arts are precise movement inside a changing reality. The ability to sense the moment before it happens, to move without hesitation, and to be present inside the action.

Martial arts are flow. A rhythm built between two forces: a dialogue of movement, distance, timing, and energy.

Sometimes it is fast and sharp, like a flash. And sometimes it is quiet, slow, and asks for softness.

Martial arts ask you to listen through the body. To move between grounding and momentum, between stability and freedom.

Over time, the movement becomes more natural than thought, and the response becomes something alive, intuitive, and free.

There is sharpness in martial arts. There is precision. And there is a particular state of mind, a state in which everything comes together in one moment of presence.

The body moves, the breath leads, and time seems to slow.

Martial arts are an art of flow: flow against mass, flow against the forces acting on us.

Why Guy Gurevich?

A former Olympic judoka

With international results, including a historic medal at the World Team Championships and an individual medal at the European Championships.

He learned the body again

Through his career he faced injuries, surgeries, and a long rehabilitation, and it was there that he learned how important it is to listen to sensation, feeling, and need.

Beyond competition

Those insights led him to study martial arts in depth, a journey after different teachers and approaches, beyond achievement and victory.

He founded Gura

A home for movement, martial arts, and improvisation in Israel: a place where struggle and resistance become a chance to learn, to play, and to grow.

A little about the dojo

Our dojo is in the heart of Tel Aviv, in the Montefiore neighborhood: a pleasant, welcoming place that is home to our community. It is a living, breathing laboratory where we study our patterns of movement and thought; a place to grow, to develop, and above all to feel at home.
